Frequently asked questions

What is GRC software?
GRC software is a platform that helps organisations manage governance, risk management and compliance in one place. It centralises risk registers, policies, controls, compliance obligations and audit evidence, and automates the reviews, approvals and reporting that connect them.
What does a GRC platform do?
A GRC platform records risks and obligations, assigns owners, routes assessments and approvals, tracks remediation and stores audit evidence. It then reports on risk exposure and compliance status so leadership can make informed decisions. TopSqill does this through configurable forms, workflow automation and SQL-based reporting.
What are the three pillars of GRC?
The three pillars are governance, risk management and compliance. Governance sets direction, policies and accountability. Risk management identifies, assesses and treats risks to objectives. Compliance ensures the organisation meets laws, regulations, standards and internal policies.
How does GRC software work?
GRC software captures information through structured forms, evaluates it against defined criteria, assigns ownership and approvals, and tracks follow-up actions to completion. Each action is logged, and dashboards give a live view of risk, compliance and audit status.
What is the difference between GRC and ERM?
Enterprise risk management (ERM) focuses on identifying and managing risks that affect strategic and business objectives. GRC is broader: it combines risk management with governance and compliance. In practice, ERM is one component of a GRC programme, and enterprise risk management software is often part of a GRC platform.
What are the benefits of GRC automation?
GRC automation reduces manual tracking, reminders and evidence chasing. It creates consistent assessments, faster approvals, clear accountability, complete audit trails and up-to-date reporting. Compliance automation software also lowers the chance of missed deadlines and outdated policies.
How does AI support GRC management?
AI can speed up repetitive GRC work: drafting policies, suggesting risk ratings, mapping controls to frameworks, classifying evidence and summarising reports. Human reviewers should validate every AI output, and authorised users should make final approval decisions.
What is AI governance?
AI governance is the set of policies, processes and controls an organisation uses to approve, monitor and manage AI systems responsibly. It covers AI use case intake, risk assessment, data protection, security, legal review and ongoing oversight. The NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001 and the EU AI Act are common references.
How does GRC software support audit readiness?
GRC software links evidence to the controls and requirements it supports, records every approval and change in an audit log, and tracks findings through to closure. When an audit begins, teams can show current evidence and history instead of collecting it from scratch.
How do organisations manage compliance across multiple frameworks?
They map each control once to every framework requirement it satisfies, such as ISO 27001, SOC 2 and GDPR. Evidence collected for that control then supports all mapped frameworks, reducing duplicate work. A GRC platform keeps these mappings, owners and test results in one place.
